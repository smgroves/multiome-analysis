# Domain-shift diagnostic: organoid/mets_compiled vs. in-vivo GEMM

Generated by `diagnose_domain_shift_6667.py`. Question: is the shGFP organoid (and
mets_compiled) external-validation shortfall explained by cell-culture/metastatic-context
stress rather than by boba-T's fitted rules failing to generalize?

## 1. Continuous stress score: weak, inconsistent signal

Per-cell `|predicted - actual|` residual for each dataset's 10 worst-validating genes,
correlated against that dataset's own precomputed continuous stress score
(`stress_score1` for organoid, `Stress_score1` for mets_compiled): 27/40 gene×dataset
pairs are nominally significant (p<0.05, large N makes this easy), but effect sizes are
tiny (|r| mostly <0.2) and **the sign is inconsistent** — about half positive, half
negative. A genuinely stress-driven confound should show a stronger, consistently
positive correlation (higher stress -> higher error) across most genes. It doesn't. The
continuous stress score, by itself, does not explain most of the external-validation
error.

## 2. Discrete subpopulation identity: a real, more specific signal

Grouping the same residuals by discrete cell-type call (organoid's `predicted.id`) or
cluster (mets_compiled's `seurat_clusters`) tells a sharper story:

- **mets_compiled, cluster `2`** (1221 cells — the 3rd largest cluster, not a small/noisy
  group) is the single highest-residual cluster for **7 of the 10** worst-validating
  genes (`FOXO3`, `RORA_RORB`, `ETS1`, `TBX15`, `KMT2A`, `ICAM1`, `SMAD3`). This is a
  concentrated, well-powered signal: whatever's driving mets_compiled's validation
  shortfall is disproportionately located in this one subpopulation, not spread evenly
  across the dataset.
- **organoid, cell-type `"Stress"`** is the highest-residual group for 4/10 genes in
  shGFP and 5/10 in shRORB2 — also enriched, but this population is small (90 cells in
  the full organoid dataset, fewer still per condition split), so this read is more
  noise-prone than mets_compiled's cluster 2 and should be treated as suggestive rather
  than conclusive on its own.

## Interpretation

This argues against a simple "the whole null condition is uniformly worse because
cultured/metastatic cells are stressed" story. Instead, it points to a **specific
subpopulation** — most clearly mets_compiled's cluster 2 — as the actual locus of
mismatch. Two consistent explanations: (a) that subpopulation is genuinely
out-of-distribution relative to the GEMM allograft data the rules were fit on (a real
domain-shift pocket, not a uniform effect), or (b) it represents a real biological state
that the 53-node/228-edge network's regulator set doesn't capture well, independent of
culture stress per se. Either way, the fix isn't "the whole shGFP/mets_compiled dataset
is untrustworthy as a validation set" — most of each dataset validates fine; a bounded
subpopulation is where the fit degrades. Worth a closer look at what distinguishes
mets_compiled's cluster 2 biologically (marker genes, `Int_score1`/`NE1_score1`/etc.
archetype scores) before drawing further conclusions.

## Caveats

- The organoid "Stress" cell-type finding is based on a small population (~90 cells
  total in the unsplit organoid dataset) and should be corroborated with a larger stress
  population before treating it as equally solid as the mets_compiled cluster-2 finding.
- This diagnostic used each dataset's own pre-existing score/cluster columns, not a new
  independent stress signature — it inherits whatever those upstream calls already
  captured or missed.

## 3. What mets_compiled's cluster 2 actually is (`characterize_mets_cluster2.py`)

Followed up on §2's "closer look" with a full differential-expression pass (whole
preprocessed gene panel, not just network genes) plus an attractor-distance check.

**It's a dissociation/acute-stress artifact signature, not a distinct tumor archetype.**
Cluster 2's top differentially-*higher* genes are dominated by canonical immediate-early
genes induced by tissue dissociation during single-cell prep: `EGR1` (+0.218, the single
largest difference in either direction), `FOS` (+0.087), `JUN` (+0.069), `FOSB` (+0.039)
— a well-documented scRNA-seq technical confound (e.g. van den Brink et al. 2017). A
combined IEG score (`EGR1`/`FOS`/`JUN`/`FOSB`/`JUND`/`JUNB` mean) is markedly higher in
cluster 2 (0.225 vs. 0.152), consistent with the dataset's own `Stress_score1` also being
elevated there (+0.037, §2). Overlaid on this stress program, cluster 2 shows **suppressed
lineage-identity TFs**: `NFIB` (-0.262), `ZBTB20` (-0.206), `ZEB1` (-0.130), `TCF4`
(-0.123), `RORB` (-0.115), `SOX11` (-0.112), `RORA_RORB` (-0.064), `TBX15` (-0.033) — most
of the network's own worst-validating genes in this dataset (`RORA_RORB`, `TBX15`, `NCAM1`,
`KMT2A`) are among these suppressed-identity genes, while others (`ICAM1`, `SMAD3`,
`NFKB1`) have technically-significant but near-zero absolute differences (both means near
0), i.e. not a biologically meaningful shift for those three specifically.

**Not an "unseen attractor."** Checked whether cluster 2's mean continuous state (network
genes only) sits distinctly closer to, or further from, any of 6667's 8 characterized GEMM
attractors (`6667/attractors/average_states.txt`: `Generalist_NE`/`Generalist_nonNE`/
`Arc_1`-`Arc_6`) than the rest of mets_compiled does. It doesn't — cluster 2's and the
rest's distances to every attractor are within ~0.15 of each other, both closest to `Arc_3`
then `Generalist_NE`, in the same rank order. So this isn't a case of a genuinely novel
biological state that a correctly-generalizing Boolean network should still resolve to a
known attractor (the claim BoBa-T makes about unseen clusters) — cluster 2 isn't
"unseen" in that sense at all. It's the *same* underlying identity as the rest of the
dataset, with an acute technical stress-response program temporarily overwriting part of
the measured transcriptional readout. A model fit on identity-driven regulatory dynamics
has no reason to predict a dissociation artifact, and arguably shouldn't be expected to —
this is a data-quality caveat for mets_compiled specifically, not a BoBa-T generalization
failure.

Outputs: `mets_cluster2_differential_expression.csv` (full gene panel),
`mets_cluster2_attractor_distances.csv`.

## 4. organoid_shGFP: correlation-structure preservation predicts R², but organoid is not a categorical outlier (`diagnose_organoid_shgfp.py`, `compare_correlation_shift_across_samples.py`)

**(a) It's not a set of hard genes — it's the organoid context.** Every one of shGFP's
worst-validating genes validates *excellently* in-sample (6667's own held-out GEMM test
set): mean in-sample R² = **0.855**, vs. mean shGFP R² = **0.023**. Zero of 53 genes are
bad in both (`organoid_shgfp_insample_vs_external_r2.csv`). `LMX1B` alone goes from
R²=0.91 in-sample to R²=-0.84 in shGFP. These genes are not intrinsically difficult for
BoBa-T to predict — the fitted rules describe them very well when the underlying data
matches the training context. This part holds up.

**(b) Correlation-structure preservation genuinely predicts R² — but as a general,
continuous, cross-sample relationship, not an organoid-specific effect.** *(Correction:
an earlier version of this section framed the organoid finding as "in-vitro vs in-vivo."
That framing doesn't survive checking against the full sample set below and has been
replaced.)* Loaded GEMM training data and organoid_shGFP through the identical
`node_normalization=0.3` pipeline and compared the Pearson correlation of every one of the
network's 226 fitted gene-regulator edges between the two datasets: 42% of all edges flip
sign, 38% of the *strong* GEMM edges (|r|>0.5) still flip. Taken alone this looked like a
smoking gun for organoid specifically. But repeating the same edge-correlation comparison
for **all 33 of 6667's other external validation samples with a scored `summary_stats.csv`**
(12 allografts, 20 human tumors, plus mets_compiled) shows organoid_shGFP's numbers
(overall edge-correlation 0.27, strong-edge sign-flip rate 0.38) are unremarkable within
that full population — several allograft and human tumor samples show similarly or more
degraded correlation preservation. What *does* hold up as a strong, general predictor
across all 33 samples: **the fraction of GEMM's strong edges that flip sign correlates
with that sample's mean R² at r=-0.72** (r=-0.68 using all edges, not just strong ones;
r=0.15 for raw correlation-of-correlations, which is noisy and not a useful single
predictor). This is not primarily a cell-count artifact (`corr(n_cells,
strong_sign_flip_rate)` = 0.12-0.26 across two runs, much weaker than the -0.72 relationship
itself).

**(c) organoid_shGFP and mets_compiled sit at the bad tail of that same population-wide
relationship — alongside the worst human tumor and allograft samples, not in a separate
category.** Bottom 8 of all 33 scored samples by mean R²:

| sample | category | strong_sign_flip_rate | mean R² |
|---|---|---|---|
| mets_compiled | mets_compiled | 0.311 | 0.019 |
| organoid_shGFP | organoid | 0.377 | 0.023 |
| human_RU1215 | human tumor | 0.358 | 0.033 |
| human_RU1108 | human tumor | 0.318 | 0.078 |
| human_RU1322 | human tumor | 0.311 | 0.097 |
| human_RU1145 | human tumor | 0.351 | 0.098 |
| organoid_combined | organoid | 0.338 | 0.100 |
| allograft_mt3 | allograft | 0.311 | 0.110 |

`human_RU1215` — a different species, unambiguously in vivo — validates barely better than
organoid_shGFP, with a comparably high strong-edge sign-flip rate. Meanwhile the *best*
allograft (`TKO-luc`, R²=0.55) and best human tumor (`RU1311`, R²=0.54) both have low
sign-flip rates (0.16, 0.25). Full table: `all_samples_corr_vs_r2.csv`.

**Bottom line, corrected: this is not primarily a BoBa-T fitting problem, and — as far as
checked — not primarily an in-vitro-vs-in-vivo effect either.** Whatever makes a given
external sample's gene-regulator correlation structure diverge from GEMM's — which varies
continuously across allografts, human tumors, *and* organoid, not by species or culture
status — is the real predictor of how well that sample validates. organoid_shGFP happens
to be one of the more extreme cases of this, on par with the single worst human tumor
sample rather than qualitatively different from the in-vivo samples. What specifically
drives any one sample's correlation-structure divergence (tumor subtype/lineage-state
composition, technical depth, something else) is still open — see §5 for how to chase
this without assuming a category (in-vitro, species, etc.) in advance.

Outputs: `organoid_shgfp_insample_vs_external_r2.csv`,
`organoid_shgfp_edge_correlation_shift.csv`, `allograft_control_summary.csv`,
`all_samples_corr_vs_r2.csv`.

## 5. Distance from the training distribution (UMAP, kNN, attractor distance) is a much weaker predictor than correlation-structure preservation (`umap_archetype_distance_analysis.py`)

Direct, assumption-free test of §4's open question, without presupposing any category
(in-vitro, species, etc.): does a sample simply being "far away" from the GEMM training
distribution predict its R², rather than (or in addition to) the specific gene-gene
correlation-structure preservation metric in §4? Pooled GEMM training cells with every
scored sample's cells (raw, comparable log1p+MAGIC-imputed units, 1500 cells/sample cap),
fit one shared UMAP embedding, and computed three distance metrics per sample against the
same 33-sample population used in §4:

| metric | corr with mean R² |
|---|---|
| strong-edge sign-flip rate (§4, for reference) | **-0.72** |
| distance to nearest of 6667's 8 known attractors | -0.49 |
| mean kNN distance to GEMM training cells, raw 53-gene space (k=15) | -0.32 |
| UMAP-space centroid distance from GEMM training centroid | -0.09 (no relationship) |

*(Corrected after an initial bug: the first pass loaded `6667/data_split/train_t0combined.csv`
with `norm=None` intending "raw GEMM data," but that file is already
`node_normalization=0.3`-clipped — confirmed directly, exactly 60% of any gene's values are
exactly 0 or 1 — so GEMM's side of the comparison was in the wrong units against every
external sample's genuinely-raw values. Fixed by loading the true raw source file
(`data/adata_imputed_combined_v3_RORA_RORB_ave.csv`), restricted to the saved training-cell-ID
list. kNN distance moved from -0.22 to -0.32; UMAP centroid distance stayed near zero either
way, -0.09 now vs. 0.05 before — still not a useful metric here.)*

All three distance metrics point the *expected* direction (farther -> lower R², except
UMAP centroid distance which shows no relationship at all — consistent with UMAP's known
unreliability for preserving global/between-cluster distances, so this one is not a
useful metric here). But none of them come close to the correlation-structure metric's
-0.72. Concretely: nearest-attractor distance barely varies across the *entire* sample
population (3.44 to 3.65 across all 33 samples) — the worst-validating samples
(`mets_compiled` 3.63, `organoid_shGFP` 3.60, `human_RU1215` 3.65) are not meaningfully
farther from a known attractor than the best (`allograft_TKO-luc` 3.52, `human_RU1311`
3.44).

**Interpretation**: whether a sample's *average expression state* resembles GEMM's (or a
known attractor) is not what predicts validation quality — a sample can have an
unremarkable, GEMM-like mean profile and still validate badly, if the specific gene-gene
covariance structure BoBa-T's rules depend on has shifted. This sharpens §4's finding
rather than replacing it: the useful direction to chase what's *driving* a sample's
correlation-structure divergence is not "how different is this sample's cell population,
overall" but something more specific to pairwise regulatory relationships — e.g.
lineage/archetype *composition* (mixture of subpopulations, not just mean position),
technical depth, or something not yet checked, rather than a simple distance-from-training
signal in expression or embedding space.

Outputs: `umap_embedding_all_samples.csv` (per-cell, for visualization),
`umap_archetype_distance_vs_r2.csv`.

## 6. Why the correlation structure shifts: restriction of range (`diagnose_sample_diversity.py`)

**Fair challenge to §4-5: "strong-edge sign-flip rate predicts R²" is close to
tautological on its own.** BoBa-T's rules *are* that correlation structure, so of course a
sample whose gene-gene relationships have shifted gets predicted poorly — that's a
restatement of the mechanism, not an explanation of *why* the structure shifts for some
samples and not others. This section tests the most direct candidate mechanism.

**Hypothesis: restriction of range.** If a sample's cells span less of the underlying
cell-state diversity that GEMM's training tumors span (fewer distinct archetypes/less
spread along the identity axes the network's genes vary along), within-sample gene-gene
correlations will attenuate or flip sign as a *statistical byproduct* of reduced variance
— independent of any real biological rewiring — and, separately but for the same
underlying reason, prediction will be poor because the fitted rules were calibrated to
distinguish states across a range of variation that sample barely explores. This would
give a real, mechanistic "why," not just a restatement.

**Result: supported.** Computed each sample's mean per-gene raw standard deviation
(pre-normalization, comparable log1p+MAGIC-imputed units) relative to GEMM training data's
own: **this "relative diversity" measure correlates with mean R² at r=0.58** — notably
higher than the UMAP/attractor distance metrics in §5, and only weakly related to the
sign-flip-rate metric itself (r=-0.23 between the two), so it is capturing something
distinct, not just re-deriving §4's number by another name. *(This ratio was computed
against the correct raw GEMM reference from the start — the correlation is scale-invariant
to the §5 bug, since every sample was divided by the same GEMM constant either way.)*

| sample | relative diversity vs. GEMM | mean R² |
|---|---|---|
| mets_compiled | 0.29 (lowest of all 33) | 0.019 |
| human_RU1215 | 0.55 | 0.033 |
| allograft_mt3 | 0.44 | 0.110 |
| ... | ... | ... |
| allograft_TKO-luc | 1.21 | 0.550 |
| human_RU1311 | **1.71 (highest of all 33)** | 0.538 |

`mets_compiled` — the single worst-validating sample — is also the single least-diverse
sample by this measure; the two best-validating samples (`TKO-luc`, `RU1311`) are among
the most diverse (both, notably, *more* diverse than GEMM's own training data — ratio >1).
**Honest caveat: this doesn't fully explain organoid_shGFP specifically** — its relative
diversity (0.73) is middling-to-high, not the lowest, yet its R² (0.023) is nearly as bad
as mets_compiled's. So low diversity is sufficient to predict bad validation but not
necessary. §7 chases the "second contributing cause" directly.

**Practical implication**: this reframes what "the network doesn't generalize to organoid"
means. It's closer to "the network can't be expected to resolve identity in a sample that
doesn't explore much of the identity-axis variation it was fit to distinguish" — a
statement about the *validation sample*, not a defect in BoBa-T's fitted rules. For the
paper, this argues for reporting/interpreting external validation scores alongside a
diversity or archetype-composition measure of the validation set itself, rather than
treating "external R²" as a single comparable number across samples that differ widely in
how much relevant biological variation they contain.

Outputs: `sample_diversity_vs_signflip_r2.csv`.

## 7. Not just how much variance, but which axis: why "orthogonal" variance isn't actually harmless (`diagnose_variance_composition.py`)

**Fair challenge to §4/§6: sign-flip-rate <-> R² is close to tautological** (BoBa-T's rules
*are* the correlation structure, so a shift there predicting poor prediction restates the
mechanism, not the cause) **and raises a real theoretical question**: if a sample has a lot
of variance orthogonal to GEMM's known identity axes, why should that matter for how well
the network predicts genes *along* those identity axes? Two candidate answers, tested
directly rather than assumed:
(a) low-diversity samples simply lack enough of the *right* variance for R² to be
well-defined regardless of model quality (a restatement of §6's finding, not new), or
(b) "orthogonal" variance isn't actually harmless because several of the network's own
genes (`JUN`, `JUND`, `JUNB`, `FOS`, `EGR1`) are simultaneously (i) targets of whatever
drives that orthogonal axis (e.g. acute stress/dissociation, per §3) and (ii) *fitted
regulators BoBa-T's rules use for other genes* (`ASCL1`, `HES1`, `LMX1B`, `TCF7L1`, and
others all have one of these as a regulator). A rule has no way to know a regulator's
value moved for a reason unrelated to the identity logic it encodes — it just reads the
value as given. So stress-driven movement in these specific genes doesn't stay
"orthogonal" to the identity axes in any way that matters to the model; it directly
corrupts the input to every downstream prediction that uses one of them as a regulator.

**Method**: fit PCA on GEMM's raw training data (correct raw file, restricted to the saved
train-cell-ID list) to define GEMM's own top-5 "identity axes" (explaining 97% of GEMM's
own variance; PC1 alone is essentially an `ASCL1`/`MEIS2` axis, PC3 is essentially an
`EGR1`/`FOS`-vs-`SOX11` axis — i.e. GEMM's own top axes already partially separate an
IEG-like direction from lineage-TF directions). Projected every external sample onto this
*fixed* basis (GEMM's own mean + loadings — not a per-sample refit) and computed, for each
sample, what fraction of *its own* total variance lands on GEMM's identity axes vs. on the
specific IEG-gene subspace.

**Result**: `identity_pc_var_frac` correlates with mean R² at **r=0.65** — the best of
every distance/composition metric tried so far, and more mechanistically specific than
§6's raw diversity number (r=0.58) since it asks not just "how much variance" but "how
much of it is on the axes that matter." `ieg_var_frac` correlates *positively* with R²
(r=0.43) — more IEG variance associates with *better* validation, which on its own does
not support "IEG variance is categorically bad." Resolving this against organoid_shGFP
specifically clarifies why:

| sample | identity_pc_var_frac | ieg_var_frac | relative diversity | mean R² |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| organoid_shGFP | 0.21 | **0.005 (lowest of all 33)** | 0.73 | 0.023 |
| mets_compiled | 0.27 | 0.13 | 0.29 | 0.019 |

**organoid_shGFP's problem is not excess IEG/stress variance crowding out identity
signal** (the hypothesis suggested by §3's mets_compiled cluster-2 finding) — its IEG
variance fraction is the *lowest* of any of the 33 samples, essentially flat. Its identity-
axis fraction is also low (0.21), and since identity (0.21) + IEG (0.005) accounts for only
~22% of its variance while its overall diversity is moderate-to-high (0.73), **most of
organoid_shGFP's variance sits on neither GEMM's identity axes nor the IEG axis** — some
other, uncharacterized source (candidates: organoid-culture-specific technical/batch
variance, a genuine biological axis not present in GEMM, or noise from a different
imputation/depth regime). `mets_compiled`, by contrast, fits the original stress-crowding
story reasonably well: low total diversity (0.29, the lowest of all 33) *and* a
meaningful share of what diversity it has is on the IEG axis (0.13, second-highest of the
bottom-8 group) — consistent with §3's direct characterization of its cluster 2.

**So the two worst-validating samples fail for different specific reasons**, both
downstream of the same general principle (§4-§7): validation quality tracks how much of a
sample's variance sits on the axes BoBa-T's rules actually use, not how much variance it
has overall, and not how far its average state sits from training data (§5).

Outputs: `variance_composition_vs_r2.csv`.

## 8. organoid_shGFP's "uncharacterized" 78% is real biology, just not aligned to GEMM's specific axes (`diagnose_organoid_shgfp_unexplained_variance.py`)

§7 found little of organoid_shGFP's variance lands on GEMM's identity axes or the IEG
axis. Two very different explanations were live: (a) that ~78% is noise/technical
artifact, or (b) it's real biological heterogeneity that simply isn't oriented the same
way as GEMM's specific PCA axes. Checked directly by fitting PCA on organoid_shGFP's *own*
raw data (its own dominant directions of variation, not GEMM's basis) and correlating each
top PC's per-cell scores against every available archetype/lineage/technical metadata
column in `organoid_clusters.csv`.

**Answer: (b). This is real, substantial, biologically-coherent heterogeneity.**
organoid_shGFP's own PC1 (47% of its variance) correlates with `prediction.score.Intermediate`
(r=0.39), `HES1_high_score1` (r=0.33), and `Ascl1_targets_score1` (r=-0.31) — an
Intermediate-vs-ASCL1/NE identity axis. PC2 (19% of variance) correlates strongly with
`NE2_score1` (r=0.64), `Rest_targets_score1` (r=0.62), `Ascl1_targets_score1` (r=0.58),
and `Intermediate_score1` (r=-0.55) — a strong NE-lineage identity axis. Categorically,
fine-grained subcluster identity (`tree.ident`, 21 groups) explains 51% of PC1's variance
and 33% of PC2's (`seurat_clusters`/`rcpa_clusters`, 8 groups, explain 34% and 24%
respectively). Together PC1+PC2 account for ~65% of organoid_shGFP's total variance and
are both cleanly, substantially explained by real lineage/archetype identity — not
technical depth (`nFeature_RNA`/`gene_count` only show up weakly, on PC3, r=0.27) and not
noise.

**So organoid_shGFP is not flat or uninformative** — it has real cell-state heterogeneity
along recognizable NE/Intermediate/ASCL1 axes, the same kind of biology GEMM's identity
axes represent. The reason so little of it landed on *GEMM's specific* PCA axes in §7 is
not that the biology is absent, but that organoid's genes covary along a differently
-oriented combination than GEMM's — the same underlying phenomenon as §4's finding that
many specific gene-gene correlations shift or flip sign between organoid and GEMM. This
ties §4, §7, and §8 into one consistent picture: the relevant lineage biology is present in
organoid_shGFP, but the *specific linear combination of genes* that expresses it differs
from GEMM's, so a rule set built on GEMM's specific combination doesn't transfer even
though both systems are exploring recognizably the same biology.

Outputs: `organoid_shgfp_own_pc_metadata_correlation.csv`,
`organoid_shgfp_own_pc_categorical_variance.csv`.
